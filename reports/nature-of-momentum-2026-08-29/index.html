Toward a principled theory of momentum

Research memo · 29 August 2026

In Muon, momentum acts through temporal averaging and matrix geometry. A temporal filter can rotate the matrix passed to the matrix-sign step and thereby change the update direction.

The NanoGPT runs show that changing one EMA helps, while six EMA states recover more of the gain. Eight states do not improve on six. The runs leave one ambiguity: the useful ingredient may be the current filter, the momentum history, or the model path created by earlier updates.

Working hypothesis. Momentum in Muon selects among matrix-valued gradient patterns with different temporal structure. Scheduling helps because the competing patterns change over training.

1. The experimental clue

Scheduling one EMA recovers about one third of the eight-state gain. The six-state K6 and eight-state K8 kernels are tied within the precision of these discovery runs. All candidates use the same Muon update through step 1000. The single-EMA decay then moves from 0.982 to 0.944. K6 and K8 combine EMA states with coefficients that change during training. The baseline is the matched bi-Maxwell control: the standard two-EMA kernel switched on at step 1000.

In a separate A100 fork, reversing the single-EMA schedule erased the gain and lost substantially to bi-Maxwell. The tested forward schedule therefore outperformed the tested reverse fork.

The loss comparison assigns no share among three possible causes: the filter used now, the momentum history carried into the comparison, and the model path created by earlier updates. The experiment in Section 4 changes only the first.

The power-law run produced average gradient ages of about 78 → 9 steps, versus its 52 → 16 target, together with much larger responses to alternating and noisy inputs. Treat this run as a failed construction. A corrected power law should follow the same-state filter test.

2. Why a temporal filter can change Muon's direction

A filter changes Muon's direction when temporal components point in different matrix directions. Write the local gradient at step t as a sum of components, where Aj is the matrix amplitude and ωj is its temporal frequency:

Gt=∑jRe⁡ ⁣(Ajeiωjt)G_t=\sum_j \operatorname{Re}\!\left(A_j e^{i\omega_j t}\right)Gt​=j∑​Re(Aj​eiωj​t)

The matrix Aj sets the component's spatial orientation. For momentum weights wk, define the frequency response as H(ω)=∑k≥0wke−iωkH(\omega)=\sum_{k\ge0}w_ke^{-i\omega k}H(ω)=∑k≥0​wk​e−iωk. Filtering gives

Zt=∑k≥0wkGt−k=∑jRe⁡ ⁣(H(ωj)Ajeiωjt)Z_t=\sum_{k\ge 0}w_kG_{t-k} =\sum_j\operatorname{Re}\!\left(H(\omega_j)A_je^{i\omega_jt}\right)Zt​=k≥0∑​wk​Gt−k​=j∑​Re(H(ωj​)Aj​eiωj​t)

Each component receives its own amplitude change and phase shift. Muon then computes

Ut=msign⁡(Zt)U_t=\operatorname{msign}(Z_t)Ut​=msign(Zt​)

For the ideal matrix sign and any positive scalar c,

msign⁡(cZ)=msign⁡(Z),c>0\operatorname{msign}(cZ)=\operatorname{msign}(Z),\qquad c>0msign(cZ)=msign(Z),c>0

A common positive rescaling of the entire filtered matrix disappears in the ideal matrix-sign normalization. Changing the ratio and phase between two differently oriented components can rotate the singular directions of their sum. For two components at frequencies ω1 and ω2, the filter changes their relative complex weight by

H(ω1)H(ω2)\frac{H(\omega_1)}{H(\omega_2)}H(ω2​)H(ω1​)​

Mean lag and total noise gain integrate over temporal frequency and discard matrix orientation. The diagnostic therefore records each time scale's contribution to the matrix directions before the matrix-sign step.

3. Curvature suggests a candidate boundary condition

Across the accepted constant-learning-rate runs, measured equilibrium curvature rises as the learning rate falls. This resembles the edge-of-stability pattern and motivates a scalar reference calculation.

The product of learning-rate multiplier and measured curvature ranges from 1.54 to 1.15, so the inverse trend is clear while the constant drifts. A scalar test asks whether the filter contributes to this inverse relation.

The scalar calculation shows how a momentum kernel could move this boundary. On one quadratic direction, let gt = λxt and consider

xt+1=axt−ηλ∑k≥0wkxt−kx_{t+1}=a x_t-\eta\lambda\sum_{k\ge0}w_kx_{t-k}xt+1​=axt​−ηλk≥0∑​wk​xt−k​

The factor a is the multiplicative weight-decay factor. At the period-two boundary, the state flips sign each step: xt+1=−xt,xt−k=(−1)kxtx_{t+1}=-x_t,\quad x_{t-k}=(-1)^kx_txt+1​=−xt​,xt−k​=(−1)kxt​. Substitution gives

−1=a−ηλ∑k≥0(−1)kwk-1=a-\eta\lambda\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^kw_k−1=a−ηλk≥0∑​(−1)kwk​

Define the response to an alternating signal as

F=H(π)=∑k≥0(−1)kwkF=H(\pi)=\sum_{k\ge 0}(-1)^k w_kF=H(π)=k≥0∑​(−1)kwk​

The period-two boundary therefore satisfies

ηλcritF=1+a\eta\lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}F=1+aηλcrit​F=1+a

For a single decay, this gives the period-two boundary. For a multi-timescale kernel, scan the other frequencies as well. If period two is the binding mode, the scalar model predicts λcrit∝F−1\lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}\propto F^{-1}λcrit​∝F−1; the same-state comparison tests that prediction.

The scalar curvature prediction disagrees with the measured curvature in this separately trained model. The next test changes filter coefficients at a shared checkpoint and measures Muon's direction before the paths diverge.

At early, middle, and late checkpoints, two unchanged controls and two intervention branches copy the model weights, learning rate, weight decay, data order, Muon state, Adam state, and every other pre-fork state. A common EMA bank stores identical filtered summaries of the shared raw gradient history. Candidate kernels are different linear combinations of that bank. At each checkpoint, every candidate is normalized using the finite gradient history available there so H(0)=∑k≥0wk=1H(0)=\sum_{k\ge0}w_k=1H(0)=∑k≥0​wk​=1; a constant gradient passes unchanged and the common scale is fixed. Hold each candidate's average lag, response to sign-alternating inputs, and response to white-noise inputs within registered tolerances. Report their drift over the fixed measurement window. The immediate branch difference then estimates the effect of the remaining temporal response.

Discovery batches choose a pair predicted to rotate Muon's matrix-sign update. At the intervention step, Uw is computed from the shared training batch and history. Independent held-out batches estimate the gradient g, Hessian ℋ, and predicted one-step loss change for that fixed update. With Uw=msign⁡(Zw)U_w=\operatorname{msign}(Z_w)Uw​=msign(Zw​), second-order expansion gives

The first term measures descent alignment; the second measures curvature cost. This estimate is a local diagnostic; the fixed continuation tests its predictive value.

The unchanged control supplies a common reference basis: decompose its pre-normalization matrix as Zref = UΣVT. Project every EMA stream and both intervention matrices into the same (U, V) coordinates, recording their in-basis and orthogonal contributions. Frobenius cosine between matrix-sign updates and principal angles between their leading subspaces measure the cross-branch rotation. This common basis shows which time scales changed influence when the update rotated.

The experiment begins with an offline response map across the three checkpoints. The pair with the clearest held-out difference then enters short continuations alongside duplicate controls. The continuation measures when the immediate direction change begins to affect sharpness and validation loss. A candidate pair advances only if the second-order predicted loss-change ordering has the same sign on every preregistered evaluation batch. Call the continuation positive only if the branch difference exceeds the loss-and-curvature spread between the two unchanged controls over the same window.

5. From an expressive basis to a small controller

If the same-state test identifies a reproducible filter effect, compress it into the smallest EMA bank that preserves that effect. Keep the user-facing control count at zero or one.

Let st contain measurements available to the optimizer: the learning rate, the measured distance from the curvature boundary, and the per-time-scale projection readouts from Section 4. Constrain the online EMA bank to a bounded response to white-noise input. If the same-state test finds useful filters, use the current gradient gt, Hessian ℋt, and candidate Muon update Uw to choose weights that minimize expected one-step loss change conditional on that state:

w∗(st)=arg⁡min⁡wE ⁣[−ηt⟨gt,Uw⟩+ηt22⟨Uw,HtUw⟩ | st]w^*(s_t)=\arg\min_w\mathbb E\!\left[ -\eta_t\langle g_t,U_w\rangle +\frac{\eta_t^2}{2}\langle U_w,\mathcal H_tU_w\rangle \,\middle|\,s_t\right]w∗(st​)=argwmin​E[−ηt​⟨gt​,Uw​⟩+2ηt2​​⟨Uw​,Ht​Uw​⟩​st​]

Treat the optimized filters at each checkpoint as observations. Test whether one measured state variable predicts most of their variation. Keep only features that predict held-out rotation and held-out loss or curvature at all three checkpoints.

At one shared checkpoint, vary alternating response F while holding the other summaries fixed. If curvature follows 1/F, include F in the stability model; otherwise prioritize matrix-direction features.

  1. Measure same-state response. Compare scalar-matched kernels at early, middle, and late checkpoints.
  2. Measure the post-fork outcome. Use a preregistered fixed window and record first-step rotation, loss, and curvature.
  3. Test the scalar stability correction. Run a separate same-state, constant-learning-rate fork that varies alternating response.
  4. Choose the state variable. Optimize expressive local kernels across checkpoints and regress their variation against measured state.
  5. Validate one implementation. Implement the smallest EMA realization and test baseline, high-learning-rate, and low-learning-rate trajectories.

Decision requested. Approve the same-state response map and its fixed continuation window. Defer another power-law fit, broad K-Maxwell tuning, and controller implementation until those measurements identify a reproducible state variable.

B. Curvature measurements and evidence limits

The steady points in the figure are (η, λ) = (0.60, 2.56), (0.77, 1.82), (1.15, 1.147), (1.30, 0.92), and (1.70, 0.675). Two lower-learning-rate runs failed the stated steady-regime criterion and were excluded.

Curvature is the median leading eigenvalue of each Muon matrix's diagonal Hessian block on a fixed batch, normalized to that matrix's step-1000 baseline. The transient window is 250 steps after a learning-rate raise or the step-1000 kernel switch, and 125 steps after a learning-rate drop. After that window, a segment is accepted when its whole-segment slope in log median curvature is within the length-scaled one-standard-deviation noise floor. All five points use 8-iteration Lanczos with the convergence correction and tail gate. Each point is one segment from one trajectory; its uncertainty describes within-run measurement variation.

The geometric-tail extrapolation and tail gate are a provisional analysis rule; calibration of its error model remains future work. The five points are descriptive results accepted under that rule.

The learning-rate multiplier also scales decoupled weight decay in this harness. The full hybrid Muon–Adam update depends on weight-norm adaptation, cross-block coupling, and finite Newton–Schulz effects, so the plot is a descriptive inverse trend. BF16 rounding and the numerical stabilizer also break the exact scale invariance of ideal matrix sign. The intervention uses the deployed implementation.

C. Statistical scope

The training comparisons use one seed and one checkpoint fork per candidate. Confirmatory claims require repeated seeds under the same registered protocol. The momentum-free curvature comparison uses a separate trajectory whose weight norms are about 0.31 times the reference. The local Taylor diagnostic omits the explicit decay term and requires a smooth local approximation; the fixed continuation evaluates whether its ranking is useful.

D. Full scalar boundary for an arbitrary kernel

Substitute a trial mode xt = rt into the scalar update from Section 3. Dividing by xt gives

r−a+ηλ∑k≥0wkr−k=0r-a+\eta\lambda\sum_{k\ge0}w_kr^{-k}=0r−a+ηλk≥0∑​wk​r−k=0

The stability boundary is the first root to reach |r| = 1 as ηλ increases. Setting r = eiφ and scanning φ handles a power law, K-Maxwell, or any other realized finite kernel. The period-two formula in the main text is the φ = π case.
