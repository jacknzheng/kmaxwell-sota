The current gradient is the wrong target for momentum

We tested whether minibatch noise and temporal drift could derive momentum's decay schedule. The measured optimum was far shorter than the record eight-buffer K-Maxwell kernel (K8) and moved in the opposite direction during training. The same estimation framework remains appropriate for factored curvature accumulation.

Result

A filter optimized to estimate the current population gradient does not explain the useful memory in K8. This rejects that specific estimand, while leaving open the possibility that momentum estimates a different persistent component of the gradient history.

Momentum kernel. The causal sequence of weights applied to present and past gradients. K8 combines eight exponential buffers; its average lag is plotted in Figure 1.

Test A fails at the early-state level and in the direction of change. The tracking optimum rises from 0.5 to 15.1 steps while K8 falls from 49.1 to 36.3 steps. At the early state, period-two alternation is 9.1 times the noise floor and using K8's lag raises the tracking error by a median factor of 1.76. The fixed protocol therefore rejects gradient tracking as the source of momentum's schedule.

The measured structure function grows approximately as \(\Delta^p\) with \(p=0.28\) and \(0.29\), rather than \(p=2\) for local linear drift or \(p=1\) for a random walk. Most non-noise change occurs at short lags and then saturates. The gradient is neither a slowly drifting signal nor the local-level process that would make one exponential buffer an optimal tracker.

What Test A actually optimized

Eight data-parallel workers produce eight microbatch gradients from consecutive chunks of the data stream before averaging. Their cross-worker variation measures finite-minibatch noise at the actual batch composition. Changes across consecutive steps measure how quickly the object being accumulated has become stale.

A geometric accumulator assigns lag \(k\) the normalized weight \(w_k=(1-\beta)\beta^k\). Its average lag is

\[ A=\sum_{k\geq0}kw_k=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}. \]

For an object with noise energy \(\sigma_t^2\) and local drift energy \(d_t^2\), the geometric accumulator has the approximate prediction error

\[ \mathcal E_t(A)=\frac{\sigma_t^2}{2A+1}+d_t^2A^2. \]

The first term rewards longer averaging. The second penalizes stale history. Their minimizer \(A_t^*\) determines the decay \(\beta_t=A_t^*/(A_t^*+1)\). Noise and drift are remeasured as training proceeds, so scheduling follows from state rather than step number.

Derivation for a trailing window and a geometric accumulator

Observe \(y_{t-k}=x_{t-k}+\varepsilon_{t-k}\), where the noise terms are independent with \(\mathbb E\lVert\varepsilon_{t-k}\rVert^2=\sigma_t^2\), and approximate local motion by \(x_{t-k}=x_t-kd_t\).

A length-\(W\) trailing average has variance \(\sigma_t^2/W\) and bias \(-(W-1)d_t/2\). Its exact local error is

\[ \mathcal E_t(W)=\frac{\sigma_t^2}{W} +\frac{d_t^2(W-1)^2}{4}. \]

Treating \(W\) as continuous, the minimizer solves \(W^2(W-1)=2\sigma_t^2/d_t^2\). For a long window,

\[ W_t^*\approx\left(\frac{2\sigma_t^2}{d_t^2}\right)^{1/3}. \]

For geometric weights, \(\sum_k w_k^2=1/(2A+1)\) and \(\sum_k kw_k=A\). The exact local objective is therefore \(\sigma_t^2/(2A+1)+d_t^2A^2\), and its optimum solves

\[ A_t^*(2A_t^*+1)^2=\frac{\sigma_t^2}{d_t^2}. \]

The rectangular and geometric rules share cube-root scaling but have different constants. We use the geometric equation because the deployed accumulator is an exponential moving average.

A steady-state Kalman filter for a Gaussian local-level random walk is also a single exponential moving average. That result and the calculation above arise from different state models, but both expose the same limitation: homogeneous noise and one drift scale produce one temporal scale. The measured failure of a single exponential buffer therefore points directly at violated assumptions.

Test B is retired with Test A

Test B would have applied the same current-gradient tracking loss separately to fixed temporal bands. Test A shows that this loss prefers nearly memoryless tracking precisely where long-memory K8 helps most. More bands would add flexibility without correcting the target, so the bandwise scorer will not be used to explain K8.

This does not reject estimand-based momentum. The current population gradient has now failed two independent tests: an earlier kernel-closeness ranking and Test A's schedule prediction. The Newton/secant direction also failed as a proposed target. A different persistent component of the gradient history remains viable until it receives an unconfounded test.

The first persistent-component test measured the controller instead

Persistent gradient component. For a window of \(W\) steps, \(p_t(W)\) is the mean of the per-step reference gradients in that window. The measured structure function has a gap between period-two alternation, fast variation that mixes by lag 2, and a component that changes little through lag 64. The test checks \(W\in\{32,64,128,256\}\) so its conclusion does not depend on one boundary inside that gap.

The archived test compared the unit-polar direction produced by each momentum kernel with the mean reference gradient over the subsequent window. Its predicted ordering was K8, bi-Maxwell, one exponential buffer, then the raw gradient. Every tested window produced the reverse qualitative result: raw gradients were approximately neutral, while kernel directions had inner products from \(-9\) to \(-36\), with half-split errors of about 1.5–2.8.

The negative values do not falsify the persistent component. The future gradients come from the trajectory driven by the update being scored. A successful controller consumes and can reverse the gradients along its descent direction, so the statistic mixes the field with the controller's response. The identical ranking across all four windows establishes this closed-loop anti-correlation, while leaving estimator alignment unresolved.

A clean test would reduce the learning rate by a factor of ten for a 256-step continuation, making the model move little while the reference-gradient window is recorded. Ranking kernels against that nearly fixed-state mean would test the persistent-component estimand with much less controller feedback. This continuation is proposed and awaits compute approval.

Replica clocks remain meaningful for factored curvature

SOAP and Shampoo accumulate left and right factors from a gradient matrix \(G_t\):

\[ L_t=G_tG_t^{\mathsf T}, \qquad R_t=G_t^{\mathsf T}G_t. \]

Replica gradients separate the mean-gradient outer product from stochastic covariance. If \(G_t^{(a)}=M_t+\varepsilon_t^{(a)}\) and the workers are averaged, then

\[ \mathbb E[\bar G_t\bar G_t^{\mathsf T}] =M_tM_t^{\mathsf T}+C_{\bar G,L,t}, \qquad C_{\bar G,L,t} =\mathbb E[(\bar G_t-M_t)(\bar G_t-M_t)^{\mathsf T}]. \]

The right factor has the analogous decomposition. Independent sampling would make the covariance term fall in proportion to inverse token count, but the measured per-direction noise falls only as tokens to the power \(-0.5\) to \(-0.65\). Consecutive-stream correlations therefore matter. The covariance contribution is measured at the training batch composition rather than extrapolated with an independence formula.

The first measurements place the factor-side optimal average lag between 1.8 and 3.0 steps. These values are provisional because the factor expectation contains a batch-size-dependent covariance term; the final clock must be recomputed after that term is measured at the training batch composition.

The factor model uses one measured temporal clock and per-direction spectral shrinkage. Held-out replicas determine how far each inverse-factor correction should move from the identity. This is the smallest extension of SOAP's working design. It remains only if held-out factor-action residuals show no reproducible temporal structure by spectral band; otherwise the factor also needs multiple clocks.

Current work and the decision sequence

Test A is rejected on the completed early and middle states. The late-state clock remains queued as a measurement of late behavior, not as a condition for this decision. Test B is retired because it inherits Test A's target.

The archived persistent-component statistic has been executed and discarded as a test because it measures closed-loop consumption. The persistent-component estimand remains open; its reduced-learning-rate continuation requires approval.

The factored accumulation calculation uses the same recordings and remains active. Once the batch-corrected factor predicts held-out action better than a fixed decay, the momentum × factored-curvature comparison asks whether response-shaped momentum and curvature accumulation can be designed separately.

Full-Hessian depth remains a bounded comparison requiring separate approval. It becomes relevant only if the factored method leaves a specific discrepancy that a deeper, cross-replicable curvature action could explain.
