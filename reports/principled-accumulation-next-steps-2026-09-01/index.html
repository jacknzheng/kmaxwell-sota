A measurable rule for accumulation

Momentum and SOAP both accumulate noisy matrices through time. Their decay rates, schedules, and regularization strengths are usually tuned independently. We propose to derive them from the same two measurements: sampling noise across data-parallel replicas and drift across nearby training steps.

Research question

Can the current training state determine how much gradient history to retain and how much factored curvature history to trust?

The candidate: replica-clocked accumulation

Eight data-parallel workers produce eight independent microbatch gradients before averaging. Their disagreement estimates finite-minibatch noise at the current model state. Changes across consecutive steps estimate how quickly the object being accumulated has become stale.

A geometric accumulator assigns lag \(k\) the normalized weight \(w_k=(1-\beta)\beta^k\). Its average lag is

\[ A=\sum_{k\geq0}kw_k=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}. \]

For an object with noise energy \(\sigma_t^2\) and local drift energy \(d_t^2\), the geometric accumulator has the approximate prediction error

\[ \mathcal E_t(A)=\frac{\sigma_t^2}{2A+1}+d_t^2A^2. \]

The first term rewards longer averaging. The second penalizes stale history. Their minimizer \(A_t^*\) determines the decay \(\beta_t=A_t^*/(A_t^*+1)\). Noise and drift are remeasured as training proceeds, so scheduling follows from state rather than step number.

Derivation for a trailing window and a geometric accumulator

Observe \(y_{t-k}=x_{t-k}+\varepsilon_{t-k}\), where the noise terms are independent with \(\mathbb E\lVert\varepsilon_{t-k}\rVert^2=\sigma_t^2\), and approximate local motion by \(x_{t-k}=x_t-kd_t\).

A length-\(W\) trailing average has variance \(\sigma_t^2/W\) and bias \(-(W-1)d_t/2\). Its exact local error is

\[ \mathcal E_t(W)=\frac{\sigma_t^2}{W} +\frac{d_t^2(W-1)^2}{4}. \]

Treating \(W\) as continuous, the minimizer solves \(W^2(W-1)=2\sigma_t^2/d_t^2\). For a long window,

\[ W_t^*\approx\left(\frac{2\sigma_t^2}{d_t^2}\right)^{1/3}. \]

For geometric weights, \(\sum_k w_k^2=1/(2A+1)\) and \(\sum_k kw_k=A\). The exact local objective is therefore \(\sigma_t^2/(2A+1)+d_t^2A^2\), and its optimum solves

\[ A_t^*(2A_t^*+1)^2=\frac{\sigma_t^2}{d_t^2}. \]

The rectangular and geometric rules share cube-root scaling but have different constants. We use the geometric equation because the deployed accumulator is an exponential moving average.

A steady-state Kalman filter for a Gaussian local-level random walk is also a single exponential moving average. That result and the calculation above arise from different state models, but both expose the same limitation: homogeneous noise and one drift scale produce one temporal scale. The measured failure of a single exponential buffer therefore points directly at violated assumptions.

The gradient candidate has to pass two tests

Test A · Does the rule explain the schedule?

The eight-buffer K-Maxwell kernel (K8), the current record kernel, shortens its average lag from approximately 49 to 36 to 29 steps across the three saved training states. Rising relative noise alone would lengthen an averaging window. Replica-clocked accumulation reproduces the observed shortening only if measured drift grows enough to dominate that effect.

The norm, drift lags, smoothing, token conversion, and geometric mapping are fixed before computing the three predictions; no fitted multiplier is allowed. A wrong ordering or a miss outside the uncertainty interval rejects this rule for the momentum schedule. Agreement would explain K8's central timescale. It would not explain K8's loss improvement.

Test B · Does the rule explain why one decay is insufficient?

K8 uses decay timescales spanning roughly 3–64 steps. We divide the dense histories into fixed octave bands with periods 2–4, 4–8, 8–16, 16–32, 32–64, and 64–128 steps. Replica noise and noise-corrected drift give one predicted \(A_t^*\) per band.

The predicted timescales must reproduce both the breadth and training-state movement of K8's fixed timescales under a declared numeric tolerance. Band boundaries are not changed after seeing the result. Agreement supports a bank of clocks; disagreement rejects this explanation of K8's shape. Even agreement does not determine the mixture weights, which require a separate held-out prediction criterion.

The same clock applies to factored curvature, with one asymmetry

SOAP and Shampoo accumulate left and right factors from a gradient matrix \(G_t\):

\[ L_t=G_tG_t^{\mathsf T}, \qquad R_t=G_t^{\mathsf T}G_t. \]

Replica gradients separate the mean-gradient outer product from stochastic covariance. If \(G_t^{(a)}=M_t+\varepsilon_t^{(a)}\) and \(r\) replicas are averaged, then

\[ \mathbb E[\bar G_t\bar G_t^{\mathsf T}] =M_tM_t^{\mathsf T}+\frac{1}{r}C_{L,t}, \qquad C_{L,t}=\mathbb E[\varepsilon_t^{(a)} \varepsilon_t^{(a)\mathsf T}]. \]

Cross-replica products estimate \(M_tM_t^{\mathsf T}\); within-replica products estimate \(C_{L,t}\). The right factor has the analogous decomposition. Recombining them at the training batch size prevents minibatch covariance from masquerading as curvature drift.

The first factor model uses one measured temporal clock and per-direction spectral shrinkage. Held-out replicas determine how far each inverse-factor correction should move from the identity. This is the smallest extension of SOAP's working design. It remains only if held-out factor-action residuals show no reproducible temporal structure by spectral band; otherwise the factor also needs multiple clocks.

Current work and the decision sequence

The pooled clock calculation is running on the serialized gradient histories. Two of the three states are being processed; the third follows. The bandwise analysis is specified and its scorer is in construction. No clock result is reported on this page yet.

The factored accumulation calculation uses the same recordings and proceeds independently of the gradient result. Once the measured factor predicts held-out action better than a fixed decay, the momentum × factored-curvature comparison asks whether the two accumulators can be designed separately.

Full-Hessian depth remains a bounded comparison requiring separate approval. It becomes relevant only if the factored method leaves a specific discrepancy that a deeper, cross-replicable curvature action could explain.
