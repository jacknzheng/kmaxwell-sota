Gradient tracking is the wrong model of momentum

We tested whether minibatch noise and temporal drift could derive momentum's decay schedule. The measured optimum was far shorter than the record kernel and moved in the opposite direction during training. The same estimation framework remains appropriate for factored curvature accumulation.

Result

A filter optimized to estimate the current population gradient does not explain the useful memory in K-Maxwell. Momentum must instead be evaluated as a response-shaping operation on an oscillatory, noisy gradient history.

Test A fails at the early-state level and in the direction of change. The tracking optimum rises from 0.5 to 15.1 steps while K8 falls from 49.1 to 36.3 steps. At the early state, period-two alternation is 9.1 times the noise floor and using K8's lag raises the tracking error by a median factor of 1.76. The fixed protocol therefore rejects gradient tracking as the source of momentum's schedule.

The measured structure function grows approximately as \(\Delta^p\) with \(p=0.28\) and \(0.29\), rather than \(p=2\) for local linear drift or \(p=1\) for a random walk. Most non-noise change occurs at short lags and then saturates. The gradient is neither a slowly drifting signal nor the local-level process that would make one exponential buffer an optimal tracker.

What Test A actually optimized

Eight data-parallel workers produce eight independent microbatch gradients before averaging. Their disagreement estimates finite-minibatch noise at the current model state. Changes across consecutive steps estimate how quickly the object being accumulated has become stale.

A geometric accumulator assigns lag \(k\) the normalized weight \(w_k=(1-\beta)\beta^k\). Its average lag is

\[ A=\sum_{k\geq0}kw_k=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}. \]

For an object with noise energy \(\sigma_t^2\) and local drift energy \(d_t^2\), the geometric accumulator has the approximate prediction error

\[ \mathcal E_t(A)=\frac{\sigma_t^2}{2A+1}+d_t^2A^2. \]

The first term rewards longer averaging. The second penalizes stale history. Their minimizer \(A_t^*\) determines the decay \(\beta_t=A_t^*/(A_t^*+1)\). Noise and drift are remeasured as training proceeds, so scheduling follows from state rather than step number.

Derivation for a trailing window and a geometric accumulator

Observe \(y_{t-k}=x_{t-k}+\varepsilon_{t-k}\), where the noise terms are independent with \(\mathbb E\lVert\varepsilon_{t-k}\rVert^2=\sigma_t^2\), and approximate local motion by \(x_{t-k}=x_t-kd_t\).

A length-\(W\) trailing average has variance \(\sigma_t^2/W\) and bias \(-(W-1)d_t/2\). Its exact local error is

\[ \mathcal E_t(W)=\frac{\sigma_t^2}{W} +\frac{d_t^2(W-1)^2}{4}. \]

Treating \(W\) as continuous, the minimizer solves \(W^2(W-1)=2\sigma_t^2/d_t^2\). For a long window,

\[ W_t^*\approx\left(\frac{2\sigma_t^2}{d_t^2}\right)^{1/3}. \]

For geometric weights, \(\sum_k w_k^2=1/(2A+1)\) and \(\sum_k kw_k=A\). The exact local objective is therefore \(\sigma_t^2/(2A+1)+d_t^2A^2\), and its optimum solves

\[ A_t^*(2A_t^*+1)^2=\frac{\sigma_t^2}{d_t^2}. \]

The rectangular and geometric rules share cube-root scaling but have different constants. We use the geometric equation because the deployed accumulator is an exponential moving average.

A steady-state Kalman filter for a Gaussian local-level random walk is also a single exponential moving average. That result and the calculation above arise from different state models, but both expose the same limitation: homogeneous noise and one drift scale produce one temporal scale. The measured failure of a single exponential buffer therefore points directly at violated assumptions.

Test B is retired with Test A

Test B would have applied the same current-gradient tracking loss separately to fixed temporal bands. Test A shows that this loss prefers nearly memoryless tracking precisely where long-memory K8 helps most. More bands would add flexibility without correcting the target, so the bandwise scorer will not be used to explain K8.

Kernel design returns to response-based questions: which temporal components change Muon's update direction, which components improve continuation loss, and why exact suppression of period-two motion was harmful. The existing frequency-response measurements constrain that work, but they do not yet define a principled kernel objective.

Replica clocks remain meaningful for factored curvature

SOAP and Shampoo accumulate left and right factors from a gradient matrix \(G_t\):

\[ L_t=G_tG_t^{\mathsf T}, \qquad R_t=G_t^{\mathsf T}G_t. \]

Replica gradients separate the mean-gradient outer product from stochastic covariance. If \(G_t^{(a)}=M_t+\varepsilon_t^{(a)}\) and \(r\) replicas are averaged, then

\[ \mathbb E[\bar G_t\bar G_t^{\mathsf T}] =M_tM_t^{\mathsf T}+\frac{1}{r}C_{L,t}, \qquad C_{L,t}=\mathbb E[\varepsilon_t^{(a)} \varepsilon_t^{(a)\mathsf T}]. \]

Cross-replica products estimate \(M_tM_t^{\mathsf T}\); within-replica products estimate \(C_{L,t}\). The right factor has the analogous decomposition. Recombining them at the training batch size prevents minibatch covariance from masquerading as curvature drift.

The first measurements place the factor-side optimal average lag between 1.8 and 3.0 steps. These values are provisional because the factor expectation contains a batch-size-dependent covariance term; the final clock must be recomputed after the replica decomposition is rescaled to the training batch.

The factor model uses one measured temporal clock and per-direction spectral shrinkage. Held-out replicas determine how far each inverse-factor correction should move from the identity. This is the smallest extension of SOAP's working design. It remains only if held-out factor-action residuals show no reproducible temporal structure by spectral band; otherwise the factor also needs multiple clocks.

Current work and the decision sequence

Test A is rejected on the completed early and middle states. The late-state clock remains queued as a measurement of late behavior, not as a condition for this decision. Test B is retired because it inherits Test A's target.

The factored accumulation calculation uses the same recordings and remains active. Once the batch-corrected factor predicts held-out action better than a fixed decay, the momentum × factored-curvature comparison asks whether response-shaped momentum and curvature accumulation can be designed separately.

Full-Hessian depth remains a bounded comparison requiring separate approval. It becomes relevant only if the factored method leaves a specific discrepancy that a deeper, cross-replicable curvature action could explain.
