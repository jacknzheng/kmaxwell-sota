Next steps toward principled accumulation

Practical second-order optimizers already show that factored curvature estimates can improve training. The unresolved problem is how long to accumulate those estimates, how strongly to trust them, and how their accumulation should interact with momentum. Today these choices are tuned. The program below tries to derive them from measured noise and drift.

The decision

Can we replace tuned decay rates in momentum and factored curvature estimation with accumulation windows and shrinkage strengths determined from the training state?

What tonight changed

The useful momentum effect is concentrated in noisy, short-timescale gradient history. In the measured NanoGPT comparison, bi-Maxwell's advantage over no momentum decreases as batch size rises and reaches measured zero at \(16\times\) batch. A single exponential moving average adds no measurable benefit at either tested batch size. Kernel shape, rather than generic averaging, carries the observed gain.

Our full-Hessian measurements cannot yet derive a useful Newton operator. Heavily damped conjugate-gradient solves agree across disjoint minibatches, but their low cost and their shallow spectral reach are the same fact: the damping suppresses the poorly estimated spectrum. Individual eigendirections replicate reliably only near the top of the spectrum. Claims that the full inverse Hessian is cheaply available, that per-rank reliability determines a fractional Newton exponent, and that momentum cannot represent Newton have therefore been withdrawn.

The hindsight-action objective has not received a fair test. Existing model snapshots are separated by 250 training steps, while the K-Maxwell kernels differ mainly over lags of roughly 3–64 steps. Sparse sampling therefore removes the temporal structure that the objective is supposed to rank. The known-ordering test failed at all three saved states, as aliasing predicts. Dense consecutive windows have now been serialized; the corrected test is running.

The pivot: estimate the curvature object that training systems use

Full-Hessian eigenvectors are an unnecessarily hard starting point for principled accumulation. SOAP, Shampoo, and related methods maintain matrix factors built from gradient statistics. For a gradient matrix \(G_t\), the basic factors have the form

\[ L_t = G_tG_t^{\mathsf T}, \qquad R_t = G_t^{\mathsf T}G_t. \]

The relevant factors are at most a few thousand dimensions in this model. Their complete eigendecompositions are practical, and their time series can be estimated directly from recorded gradients. This changes the statistical problem: instead of guessing which fragments of an enormous Hessian are trustworthy, we can measure the noise and drift of the exact objects used by a deployable optimizer.

For either factor \(S_t\in\{L_t,R_t\}\), accumulation over \(W\) steps reduces sampling variance while increasing staleness. Under a local linear-drift approximation, the leading error terms scale as

\[ \mathcal E(W) \approx \frac{\sigma_t^2}{W} + \frac{d_t^2W^2}{4}, \qquad W_t^* = \left(\frac{2\sigma_t^2}{d_t^2}\right)^{1/3}, \]

where \(\sigma_t^2\) is the measured finite-minibatch variance and \(d_t\) is the measured per-step drift of the factor. This formula is a candidate rule, not a conclusion: disjoint minibatches estimate the first term, neighboring dense checkpoints estimate the second, and held-out factor prediction tests the resulting window. The same held-out test determines how far each spectral correction should be shrunk toward the identity.

Core deliverable

A state-dependent accumulation rule for the factored metric, with its window and spectral shrinkage determined by disjoint-minibatch prediction rather than a training-step schedule.

The immediate path

The dense hindsight test finishes first because it decides whether the old objective remains useful. Its outcome does not control the main pivot. The factored-statistic study follows either way and is now the shortest route to a principled accumulation rule.

The momentum interaction is tested only after the factored metric is estimated well enough to survive held-out prediction. A negligible interaction would simplify the theory: temporal filtering handles gradient history while factored curvature handles within-step geometry. A measurable interaction would tell us which temporal degrees of freedom curvature preconditioning makes redundant.

Full-Hessian depth remains a bounded comparison, not the center of the program. It earns additional compute only if the factored study leaves a specific, measurable discrepancy that deeper curvature could explain.
