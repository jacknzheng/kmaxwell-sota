Can future local dynamics choose a momentum kernel?

Research proposal · 31 August 2026

Scheduled K8—a momentum kernel built from eight exponential buffers—beats bi-Maxwell, while a single exponential moving average provides no measurable benefit over no momentum. Bi-Maxwell's benefit shrinks as batch size grows. The useful part of momentum therefore depends on temporal shape and on gradient noise; plain averaging does not explain it. This motivates a control problem: which update should the recent gradient history produce at this state?

Proposal

Use future gradients and Hessians offline to define the locally best update. Then derive the unique causal momentum kernel whose Muon update best matches that action. The proposal succeeds only if a kernel fitted in one part of training predicts held-out kernel rankings, K8's annealing direction, and short continuation loss.

1. A hindsight action supplies the target

This section defines the action that the momentum kernel will try to reproduce and states the assumption that makes the exercise informative.

At training step \(t\), let \(u\) be the descent matrix in the update \(\theta\leftarrow\theta-u\). For each of the next \(N\) recorded states, let \(g_{t+h}\) be the gradient, \(H_{t+h}\) the Hessian, and \(\omega_h\ge0\) a declared weight. Define the hindsight action \(u_t^*\) by

\[ u_t^*=\underset{u}{\operatorname{argmin}}\left[\sum_{h=0}^{N-1}\omega_h\left(-\langle g_{t+h},u\rangle+\frac12u^\top H_{t+h}u\right)+\frac{\delta}{2}\lVert u\rVert^2\right]. \]

The damping coefficient \(\delta\) is chosen so that \(B_t=\sum_{h=0}^{N-1}\omega_hH_{t+h}+\delta I\) is positive definite. The objective is then strongly convex, and its unique solution is

\[ u_t^*=B_t^{-1}\sum_{h=0}^{N-1}\omega_hg_{t+h}. \]

This action rewards descent that persists across the future window and penalizes movement in directions with sustained positive curvature. It therefore describes a controller, rather than an estimate of the current gradient.

The horizon \(N\), future weights \(\omega_h\), and damping rule define the controller target. They must be fixed on the fitting blocks before any held-out kernel ranking is inspected. The first analysis should compare a small declared set of horizons; instability across that set would reject the target before training runs begin.

Assumption under test

Gradients and Hessians recorded along the baseline trajectory must preserve the ranking of nearby counterfactual kernels within a training phase. Fitting on earlier blocks and predicting later blocks tests this assumption. Short training continuations provide the final check.

The calculation is an offline design expense. A matrix-free solve that takes \(q\) Krylov iterations over \(N\) future Hessians costs \(qN\) Hessian–vector products per state. Direct optimization within a \(K\)-parameter kernel family instead costs \(O(KN)\) Hessian–vector products to form the small quadratic.

2. The controller target determines one causal kernel

This section turns the hindsight action into a deployable momentum rule while respecting the information discarded by Muon's matrix sign.

Let \(G_{t-\tau}\) be the gradient matrix observed \(\tau\) steps ago. In Muon's fixed Euclidean coordinates, the gradient matrix is the unique matrix whose Frobenius inner product with any step \(v\) equals the directional derivative in direction \(v\). This identifies gradient covectors and step matrices for the regression; Section 4 shows how the two underlying types transform. A length-\(L\) causal kernel assigns one weight \(w_\tau\) to each available lag:

\[ M_t(w)=\sum_{\tau=0}^{L-1}w_\tau G_{t-\tau},\qquad \sum_{\tau=0}^{L-1}w_\tau=1. \]

Muon uses the polar factor of \(M_t\): the semi-orthogonal matrix returned by matrix sign. To compare nearby matrices smoothly, define the regularized polar map

\[ P_\varepsilon(M)=M(M^\top M+\varepsilon I)^{-1/2},\qquad\varepsilon>0. \]

Let \(\mathcal J_t\) be the Jacobian of \(P_\varepsilon\) evaluated at \(u_t^*\). The product \(\mathcal J_tE\) is the first-order change in the polar update caused by a small matrix error \(E\). This Jacobian may have a null space: changing singular values without changing singular vectors can leave the polar factor unchanged to first order. Penalizing those invisible changes would optimize details that Muon discards.

For fitting states \(\mathcal T\), choose the kernel that minimizes the squared change in Muon's polar update:

\[ F(w)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\left\lVert\mathcal J_t\bigl(M_t(w)-u_t^*\bigr)\right\rVert_2^2+\xi\lVert w\rVert_2^2,\qquad \xi>0. \]

Every data-fit term is quadratic and positive semidefinite in \(w\); the ridge term is strictly convex. The normalization constraint is affine, so exactly one kernel minimizes \(F\).

Separate kernels can be fitted for early, middle, and late training. A quadratic penalty on changes between adjacent phase kernels produces a unique schedule. The resulting lag weights can then be approximated by the smallest practical exponential mixture whose held-out score is unchanged.

Required retrodiction

Without using validation losses during fitting, the functional must rank scheduled K8 above bi-Maxwell, and bi-Maxwell above both single-EMA momentum and no momentum. It must also predict the direction of K8's annealing. Failure on either test ends this formulation.

3. Momentum and Newton must be composed

This section defines the object we need to choose: an ordered pipeline of temporal accumulation, curvature estimation, preconditioning, and matrix sign.

Momentum combines gradients across time. A Newton preconditioner converts a gradient into a step by applying an inverse curvature operator. These operations can be performed in different orders, and the orders differ whenever curvature changes or is estimated with noise. Section 4 defines the mathematical types and lists the resulting pipelines.

Compare complete pipelines against the hindsight action \(u_t^*\). Hold the recorded parameter states and the high-token gradients and Hessians used to define \(u_t^*\) fixed. For each replicate \(r\), redraw the finite-token minibatches used by pipeline \(p\), call the matrix entering its final matrix-sign operation \(x_{p,r}\), and compare the resulting step with the polar-normalized hindsight action. The error is

\[ e_{p,r}=P_\varepsilon(x_{p,r})-P_\varepsilon(u_t^*). \]

Three scalar measurements describe each pipeline:

\[ \operatorname{bias}_p^2=\left\lVert\mathbb E_r[e_{p,r}]\right\rVert_2^2,\qquad \operatorname{variance}_p=\mathbb E_r\left\lVert e_{p,r}-\mathbb E_r[e_{p,r}]\right\rVert_2^2, \qquad \operatorname{MSE}_p=\mathbb E_r\lVert e_{p,r}\rVert_2^2. \]

The identity \(\operatorname{MSE}_p=\operatorname{bias}_p^2+\operatorname{variance}_p\) checks the measurement. Total error chooses the pipeline; the other two scalars explain whether it loses through systematic staleness or finite-token noise.

4. Types determine which compositions are legal

This section defines gradients as covectors, uses their transformation law to constrain the pipeline, then derives the curvature-averaging window.

A covector is a linear function that maps a possible parameter step to a scalar. The gradient \(g\) is the covector defined by the directional derivative: for a small step \(v\),

\[ Df(\theta)[v]=g^\top v. \]

This definition explains how gradients change under a reparameterization. If \(\theta'=S\theta\) for an invertible matrix \(S\), then a step transforms as \(v'=Sv\), while the gradient transforms in the opposite way:

\[ g'=S^{-\top}g,\qquad {g'}^\top v'=g^\top v. \]

Thus steps are vectors and gradients are covectors. Covectors may be added to covectors, and vectors may be added to vectors. A curvature inverse \(A^{-1}\) converts a gradient covector into a step vector. Adding a raw gradient to a preconditioned gradient requires an explicit identification such as the Frobenius choice in Section 2, and the result changes under reparameterization. Composition keeps the conversion point explicit.

Neural-network parameters use a fixed affine coordinate system, which canonically identifies gradients from different training steps by translation. Ordinary momentum uses this identification when it adds historical gradients. On a nonlinear reparameterization, that implicit transport rule changes, as the transformation law above shows.

Let \(A_s\) be a positive-definite curvature metric at step \(s\), let \(g_s\) be the gradient covector, and let \(\alpha_s\) be the temporal weight assigned to that step. Four expressions recur:

ExpressionMeaning
\(\bar A_t^{-1}\bar g_t\)Accumulate curvature and gradients separately, then precondition the accumulated gradient with the current smoothed metric.
\(\sum_s\alpha_sA_s^{-1}g_s\)Precondition each gradient with its same-step unsmoothed metric, then accumulate step vectors.
\(\sum_s\alpha_s\bar A_t^{-1}g_s\)The first expression expanded as a sum when \(\bar g_t=\sum_s\alpha_sg_s\); it is the same pipeline.
\(\sum_s\alpha_s\bar A_s^{-1}g_s\)Precondition each gradient with the smoothed metric available at its own step, then accumulate step vectors.

With a fixed positive-definite metric, all four expressions coincide. When the metric changes, the first expression converts every historical gradient using the current metric; the fourth preserves the step vector implied by the metric available when each gradient was observed. The second additionally inverts an unsmoothed metric at every step. Because inversion is operator convex on positive-definite matrices, this tends to amplify noise in low-curvature directions. This argument concerns noise in the metric alone; correlation between a same-minibatch metric and gradient can change the total error. The pipeline MSE from Section 3 therefore makes the final comparison.

A longer metric window reduces sampling noise but uses older curvature. For a trailing rectangular window of \(W\) steps, assume local linear drift of size \(d_M\) per step and independent same-step estimation variance \(\sigma_M^2\), measured in one matrix distance. The leading mean-squared error and its minimizer are

\[ E(W)=\frac{\sigma_M^2}{W}+\frac{d_M^2W^2}{4},\qquad W^*=\left(\frac{2\sigma_M^2}{d_M^2}\right)^{1/3}. \]

Estimate \(\sigma_M^2\) from independent token subsets at the same parameters. Estimate \(d_M\) from adjacent checkpoints after subtracting that same-step noise floor. Existing curvature overlap rises from about \(0.24\) early to \(0.86\) late over 250 steps, which suggests a longer late window. A numeric schedule requires noise and drift measured in the same distance; overlap alone does not determine it.

Within this architecture, apply Muon's matrix sign after temporal aggregation and curvature transformation. Accumulating already-polarized updates defines a separate optimizer.

5. The shortest decisive experiment

This section orders inexpensive failure tests before any broad training sweep.

  1. Fit the controller target. At early, middle, and late checkpoints, compute \(u_t^*\) for several future horizons and check whether its held-out ranking is stable.
  2. Derive causal kernels. Solve the convex kernel problem on blocked intervals. Report polar-visible fit error and the residual after projection into practical exponential mixtures.
  3. Attempt the known retrodictions. Predict K8 \(>\) bi-Maxwell \(>\) single EMA \(\approx\) no momentum, K8's annealing direction, and weaker temporal weighting as batch size increases.
  4. Measure the curvature window. Express same-step noise and cross-step drift in one distance, calculate \(W^*(t)\), and compare the first and fourth orderings.
  5. Run continuations for survivors. Fork from identical checkpoints and test whether predicted rankings persist in realized loss. Duplicate controls measure ordinary run divergence.
Keep or kill

Continue only if blocked fits recover both the known kernel ordering and the direction of K8's schedule, and those predictions survive short continuations. Otherwise the future local quadratic is not a useful design target for momentum, even though its minimizer is unique.

Status. The single-EMA and batch-size results motivate the controller framing. The controller functional itself has not yet passed its retrodiction tests.
